Thought Leadership Enhancements

The article was already written, already researched, and already boring. Ten enhancements, switchable one at a time, and an honest note on why each one earns its place.

The problem

When it comes to thought leadership, text content matters. The problem si that readers are not always in the right mental or physical setting for longform, well-reasoned text pieces. As most thought leadership content evolves to accommodate robot readers, this project was focused on accommodating human ones. That means skimmers.

The content

Financial services and FinTech, healthcare, legal, staffing, legal and a slew of other industries still need well reasoned thought leadership that’s indexable and easy to consume. But even smart, patient executive audiences and decision makers doing research benefit from content that makes its point and doesn’t overstay its welcome.

According to Infogram’s 2026 roundup of platform data, interactive visuals outperform static text content in a number of metrics including recall and share rate.

The solution

This was more than a graphical overhaul. In an age where advanced interactivity bcomes more accessible to create by content creators and is no longer the exclusive domain of programmers, I made a habit of asking “What would make this easier and even enjoyable to understand?” as an open-ended question, not a multiple choice selection of pre-defined web forms. The end result features an array of accessibility- and interactivity-driven elements embedded in the otherwise longform article. They include:

  • a “listen to this article” audio version that is rapidly generated through a text-to-speech platform. Some users are going to consume your content via audio whether you mean for them to or not, and this goes a very feasible extra mile to design and provide it.
  • Embedded summary slideshows that not only compress the message into a social-friendly overview, but create an interest signal in the form of an optional download.
  • Alternating background sections help with variety and make the piece go by faster.
  • Glossary pop-ups allow readers to get quick definitions for industry jargon and new (potentially branded) terminology and add to perceived authority while helping readers to learn and keeping them on-page.

The prototype

Try the prototype in this article. You can toggle on the features you want to see, or turn them all on in one click. This is not to say that every blog or case study or POV needs to include all elements here. But the end result is a different beast entirely from the wall of text we started with. It’s more welcoming, accommodating, and a more effective delivery of web content for those in the business of changing minds.
