“We need a content strategy for LinkedIn.”

→

We need somebody posting on LinkedIn.

“Can you own the content strategy piece?”

→

Can you write the copy?

Every one of these is a reasonable request. None of them is a content strategy.

Believe it or not, content strategy has a meaning.

The plan that covers the purpose, the ownership, the structure, the publication of content that meets users' needs and accomplishes business goals.

In other words, the what, who, where and (crucially) the why.

Adapted from Anna Kaley, Nielsen Norman Group.

There is a formal craft.

Content strategy doesn't need to be the practice of making decisions on the fly. There are tools, frameworks and workflows that help you make strategic decisions around content, and even help you make decisions about making decisions.

They're only a google away, but they're scattered across the internet.

Enter the Content Strategy Library

That's why I created the Content Strategy Library, mission control for people making decisions about content. It's a free compilation of formal tools and frameworks that content strategists (and the rest of us) use to figure out how to connect content, purpose, and people.
