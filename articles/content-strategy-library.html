Everyone has a content strategy. Are they talking about the same thing?

Executives talk about it. Marketing talks about it. Biz devs talk about it. The loudest person in the room gets credit for having a strong content strategy because they used the term “content strategy.”

Here’s where you’ve heard it... and what they meant.

“What’s our content strategy for this blog post?”→How are we promoting this on social?
“Where do we keep our content strategy so I can know what’s coming up?”→I’d like a link to our content calendar.
“What’s the content strategy for rolling this out?”→Is there GTM documentation and a clear content schedule?
“We need a content strategy for LinkedIn.”→It doesn't feel like posted on LinkedIn often enough, or on topic enough.
“Can you own the content strategy piece?”→Can you write the copy? 😭

Every one of these is a reasonable request. None of them is a content strategy.

Believe it or not, content strategy has a meaning.

The plan that covers the purpose, the ownership, the structure, the publication of content that meets users’ needs and accomplishes business goals.

In other words, the what, who, where and (crucially) the why.

There is a formal craft.

Content strategy doesn’t need to be the practice of making decisions on the fly. There are tools, frameworks and workflows that help you make strategic decisions around content, and even help you make decisions about making decisions.

They’re only a google away, but they’re scattered across the internet.

Enter the Content Strategy Library

That’s why I created the Content Strategy Library, mission control for people making decisions about content. It’s a free compilation of formal tools and frameworks that content strategists (and the rest of us) use to figure out how to connect content, purpose, and people.

Browse the tools

The tool library hosts a broad selection of tools to help point you in the right direction. Starting at the start? Try Content Pillars/Themes. Researching what’s out there vs. what you’ve got? Content Audit and Competitive Gap Analysis may help you get started.
